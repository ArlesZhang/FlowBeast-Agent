## Camera Movement Philosophy

### Overall Approach
- **Predominantly static** (79% of shots) — focus on performance, not camera gymnastics
- **Subtle movements** — push-ins for emotional intensity, tracking for character movement
- **No handheld** — stability, control, period drama aesthetic
- **No extreme angles** — eye-level or slightly above, natural perspective

### Movement Motivations
Every camera movement must serve the story:
- **Push-in:** Increasing emotional intensity, drawing viewer closer
- **Tracking:** Following character movement, maintaining spatial awareness
- **Pan:** Revealing space, establishing context
- **Static:** Focusing on performance, allowing emotion to breathe

## Technical Specifications

### Lens Choices
- **35mm:** Wide shots, establishing, both characters
- **50mm:** Medium shots, natural perspective, dialogue
- **85mm:** Close-ups, portraits, emotional moments
- **100mm:** Extreme close-ups, details (hands, eyes)

### Camera Height
- **Eye-level:** 90% of shots (natural perspective)
- **Slightly above:** 10% of shots (S01, S47 — establishing)
- **Low angle:** Detail shots only (S08, S21, S43 — hands)

### Depth of Field
- **Shallow:** Close-ups (focus on subject, blur background)
- **Moderate:** Medium shots (subject sharp, background soft)
- **Deep:** Wide shots (everything in focus)

### Frame Rates
- **24 fps:** All shots (cinematic feel)
- **No slow motion:** Real-time performance
- **No fast motion:** Natural pacing
